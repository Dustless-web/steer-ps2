# Message and evidence

Preserving functional strength and fat-free tissue is an important aspect of health that cannot be measured by scale weight alone.

In urban Indian health discussions, dietary weight management often fixates strictly on numbers on the bathroom scale, inadvertently ignoring the concurrent loss of non-fat mass and functional strength. Clarifying the evidence helps shift attention from rapid weight drops toward long-term functional vitality without prescriptive pressure.

## Claims

### claim_1 — PDF page 1
Adding resistance exercise to dietary weight loss protects against the loss of fat-free mass with moderate certainty and increases fat mass loss with high certainty.

> resistance exercise protected against the loss of fat- free mass (between- group standardised mean difference (SMD): 0.40, p=0.0003, moderate certainty) and increased loss of fat mass (SMD: −0.36, p<0.00001, high certainty).

Population: Adults aged 18–65 years living with overweight or obesity undergoing dietary weight loss
Limitations: Fat-free mass includes water, organs, and bone, not just muscle; statistical prediction intervals crossed zero.

### claim_2 — PDF page 1
Muscular strength is significantly greater when resistance exercise is added to dietary weight loss, based on low certainty evidence.

> Muscular strength was also significantly greater (SMD 2.36, p=0.00001) following the inclusion of resistance exercise (low certainty).

Population: Adults aged 18–65 years living with overweight or obesity undergoing dietary weight loss
Limitations: Certainty was rated low because of substantial statistical heterogeneity across trials and small study cohorts.

### claim_3 — PDF page 1
The addition of resistance exercise to dietary weight loss had no statistically significant effect on overall body mass reduction.

> Overall, resistance exercise during diet- induced weight loss had no effect on body mass (mean difference between groups: −0.32 kg, p=0.35)

Population: Adults aged 18–65 years living with overweight or obesity undergoing dietary weight loss
Limitations: A lack of statistically significant difference does not demonstrate proof of absolute equivalence.

### claim_4 — PDF page 10
Subgroup analysis showed fat-free mass preservation reached statistical significance in interventions of five months or less, but not in longer trials.

> Fat- free mass preservation was only present in shorter duration interventions (<5 months). While declining adherence over time may contribute to this finding, other factors are also likely involved.

Population: Adults aged 18–65 years living with overweight or obesity undergoing dietary weight loss
Limitations: Fewer long-term studies and higher attrition limited statistical power, which does not prove benefits disappear over time.

## Study limitations
- Allocation concealment and outcome assessor blinding were rarely reported, leaving risk of bias unclear or high across most trials.
- Fifteen of the 25 included trials enrolled only female participants, limiting generalisability to men.
- Interventions lasting six months or longer had high attrition (25% to 49%) and inconsistent reporting of exercise adherence.
- Evidence for muscular strength and blood lipid markers was rated as low certainty due to high heterogeneity and small sample sizes.
